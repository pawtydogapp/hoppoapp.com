When is my child ready for the potty?

Most children are ready to start between 18 months and 3 years, often around 2. Every child is different, and there is no need to rush.

What is common

  • Accidents are normal and part of learning.
  • Some children tell you when they need to go, and some do not. Both are normal.
  • Signs of readiness include staying dry for 2 hours, knowing when they wee or poo, disliking a wet nappy and following simple requests. Not all signs need to be there.
  • Being dry at night usually comes months or even years after being dry in the day. Bedwetting is common even at school age.

What can help

  • Get your child used to the potty first with books, a doll or sitting practice.
  • Pick a calm time to start, not during a move or the arrival of a new baby.
  • Switch to pants in the day. Keep nappies for naps and nights for now.
  • Offer the potty after waking, after meals, before going out and before sleep. If nothing happens after 3 to 5 minutes, let your child get up.
  • Praise the effort, not just the result. Clean up accidents calmly.
  • Choose clothes that are easy to pull down.

Talk to your doctor, midwife or health visitor if

  • Your child is constipated: hard, pebble-like poos or straining. Treat this before stopping nappies.
  • Progress is very slow, or your child is still in nappies when school is getting close.
  • Your child was dry but has started wetting often again.

This guide is general information, not medical advice. Trust your instinct. If you are worried, contact your doctor, midwife or health visitor. This guide is checked against the NHS, the World Health Organization (WHO), the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) and other public health sources.
