My baby wakes up a lot at night

Night waking is normal for babies. Sleep changes a lot in the first year, and every baby is different.

What is common

  • Newborns sleep about 14 to 17 hours a day in short stretches and wake to feed.
  • From 3 to 6 months, some babies sleep 5 hours or more at night. Many do not, and that is normal too.
  • From 4 to 11 months, most babies sleep about 12 to 16 hours a day, including naps.
  • Growth spurts, teething and illness can change sleep for a while.

What can help

  • Show your baby the difference between day and night: daylight and play in the day, low light and quiet voices at night.
  • Start a short, calm bedtime routine, such as a bath, pyjamas, a story and a cuddle.
  • Rest when your baby sleeps, and share night duties when you can.
  • Log sleep in Care, Sleep. It helps you see the pattern over a week.

Talk to your doctor, midwife or health visitor if

  • You are exhausted and finding it hard to cope.
  • You would like help building a routine.
  • Your baby seems unwell, or sleep changes suddenly with other symptoms.

This guide is general information, not medical advice. Trust your instinct. If you are worried, contact your doctor, midwife or health visitor. This guide is checked against the NHS, the World Health Organization (WHO), the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) and other public health sources.
