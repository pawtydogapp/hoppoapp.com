I am going back to work

Going back to work after time with your child brings many feelings at once. Guilt, worry, relief and excitement are all normal, often on the same day.

What is common

  • Many children take a few weeks to settle in a new childcare setting. Tears at drop-off are common and often stop soon after you leave.
  • Children in childcare often catch many colds and stomach bugs in the first year.
  • The first weeks back can feel very tiring as everyone gets used to new routines.
  • You can keep breastfeeding when you go back to work if you wish. Many parents feed before and after work and express milk during the day.

What can help

  • Ask your childcare how they settle new children. Most start with short visits that get longer over time. Try to begin this before your first day back.
  • Say a short, warm goodbye and leave. Ask the carer to send you a message once your child has settled.
  • If you want to keep breastfeeding, tell your employer before you return and ask for a private place and time to express. Practise expressing a few weeks before.
  • Store expressed milk at the back of the fridge. Use it within 3 days, or freeze it if you will not use it by then. Label each portion with the date.
  • Make a plan for days when your child is sick and cannot go to childcare. Think about who could help, such as a partner, family or friends.
  • Keep some time for yourself each week, even 30 minutes, and keep your evenings simple at first.

Talk to your doctor, midwife or health visitor if

  • You feel low, anxious or unable to cope for more than 2 weeks. See the guide on low mood.
  • Worry about your child or your work stops you sleeping or enjoying anything.
  • You are worried about how your child is settling after several weeks.

Get urgent help now if

  • You have thoughts of harming yourself or your baby. Get help now: call your emergency number or a crisis line, or go to the nearest emergency department.

Call your local emergency number or go to the nearest emergency department.

This guide is general information, not medical advice. Trust your instinct. If you are worried, contact your doctor, midwife or health visitor. This guide is checked against the NHS, the World Health Organization (WHO), the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) and other public health sources.
