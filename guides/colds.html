My baby has a cold

Young children catch many colds, especially in their first years of daycare. Most get better on their own.

What is common

  • A runny or blocked nose, sneezing, a cough and a slightly raised temperature are common.
  • In babies, a cold can turn into bronchiolitis, with a cough, faster breathing and trouble feeding. It is usually worst on days 3 to 5.
  • A cough can last up to 3 weeks.

What can help

  • Offer smaller feeds more often, and extra drinks for older children.
  • Keep your baby upright when awake to make breathing easier.
  • Check on your child regularly, including at night.
  • Keep the home smoke-free.
  • Saline drops can help a blocked nose. Do not use medicated nose drops or cold medicines without asking a doctor.
  • Wash hands often and keep newborns away from people with colds.

Talk to your doctor, midwife or health visitor if

  • The cold is getting worse, or your child is not their usual self.
  • Your baby feeds or drinks much less than usual.
  • There has been no wet nappy for 12 hours or more.
  • Your child is over 3 months with a temperature of 39°C or higher.

Get urgent help now if

  • Your baby is under 3 months with a temperature of 38°C or higher.
  • Your child struggles to breathe, grunts, wheezes, has flaring nostrils, or the skin pulls in under the ribs.
  • Pauses in breathing.
  • Blue or grey skin, lips or tongue.
  • Your child is floppy or cannot stay awake.

Call your local emergency number or go to the nearest emergency department.

This guide is general information, not medical advice. Trust your instinct. If you are worried, contact your doctor, midwife or health visitor. This guide is checked against the NHS, the World Health Organization (WHO), the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) and other public health sources.
