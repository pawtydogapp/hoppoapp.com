I have felt low since my baby was born

Feeling tearful, anxious or irritable in the first days after birth is very common. It is called the baby blues and usually passes within 2 weeks.

What is common

  • Postnatal depression is common and treatable. It can start in pregnancy or any time in the first year.
  • Signs include low mood, not enjoying things, feeling unable to cope, guilt, constant worry, poor sleep even when you can rest, and trouble bonding with your baby.
  • Partners and fathers can get depressed after a birth too.

What can help

  • Talk to someone you trust about how you feel.
  • Accept practical help with meals, chores or night feeds. A partner can take a night feed so you can sleep.
  • Rest when you can, and get outside for a short walk.
  • Join a parent and baby group. Many parents feel the same.
  • Avoid using alcohol to cope.

Talk to your doctor, midwife or health visitor if

  • The low feelings last more than 2 weeks or get worse.
  • You find it hard to cope day to day.
  • You have only some of the signs. Help works, and the earlier, the better.

Get urgent help now if

  • You have thoughts of harming yourself or your baby. Get help now: call your emergency number or a crisis line, or go to the nearest emergency department.

Call your local emergency number or go to the nearest emergency department.

This guide is general information, not medical advice. Trust your instinct. If you are worried, contact your doctor, midwife or health visitor. This guide is checked against the NHS, the World Health Organization (WHO), the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) and other public health sources.
