How do I protect my child in the sun?

Young skin burns easily. Shade, clothing and sunscreen together keep children safe in the sun.

What is common

  • Babies under 6 months should be kept out of direct sun. Sunscreen is not recommended at this age.
  • The sun is strongest in the middle of the day, roughly 11:00 to 15:00.
  • Children get hot and thirsty faster than adults.

What can help

  • Use shade, a parasol or a sunshade. Do not cover the pram with a blanket, which can cause overheating.
  • Dress your child in light, loose clothes, a wide-brimmed hat and sunglasses.
  • From 6 months, use sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher that protects against UVA and UVB. Reapply every 2 hours and after time in water.
  • Offer extra feeds to babies, and water to older babies and children.
  • Never leave a child in a parked car.

Talk to your doctor, midwife or health visitor if

  • Your child has sunburn with blisters, or seems unwell after time in the sun.

Get urgent help now if

  • Signs of heatstroke: very hot skin, confusion, fast breathing, floppiness, or not responding normally.

Call your local emergency number or go to the nearest emergency department.

This guide is general information, not medical advice. Trust your instinct. If you are worried, contact your doctor, midwife or health visitor. This guide is checked against the NHS, the World Health Organization (WHO), the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) and other public health sources.
