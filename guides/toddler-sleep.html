My toddler will not go to bed

Bedtime battles and night waking are common between 1 and 5 years. A calm routine and patience help most children.

What is common

  • Toddlers need about 11 to 14 hours of sleep a day, including naps. Children aged 3 to 5 need about 10 to 13 hours.
  • Getting up again, calling out and fear of the dark are common.
  • Big changes, such as a new sibling or starting daycare, can upset sleep for a while.

What can help

  • Keep a calm routine at the same time every night: bath, pyjamas, teeth, one story, goodnight.
  • Start the routine about 30 minutes before your child usually falls asleep, then move bedtime earlier slowly.
  • Turn screens off 30 to 60 minutes before bed.
  • If your child gets up, take them back to bed calmly and briefly, every time.
  • A night light or a favourite toy can help with fears.
  • Avoid long naps late in the afternoon.

Talk to your doctor, midwife or health visitor if

  • Sleep problems are affecting the whole family.
  • Your child snores loudly or seems to stop breathing for short periods while asleep.
  • You need support with a sleep plan.

This guide is general information, not medical advice. Trust your instinct. If you are worried, contact your doctor, midwife or health visitor. This guide is checked against the NHS, the World Health Organization (WHO), the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) and other public health sources.
