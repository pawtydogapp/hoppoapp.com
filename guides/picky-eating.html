My toddler is a picky eater

It is perfectly normal for toddlers to refuse new foods or eat very little at some meals.

What is common

  • Appetite changes from day to day. Look at what your child eats over a week, not one meal.
  • A child who is active, growing and well is usually getting enough.
  • Some foods need many tries before a child accepts them, and tastes change over time.

What can help

  • Eat together and eat the same food. Children learn by copying.
  • Offer small portions and praise any eating, even a little.
  • If food is refused, take it away calmly and offer it again another day.
  • Limit snacks to about 2 healthy ones a day, so your child is hungry at meals.
  • Try the same food in a new way, such as raw grated carrot instead of cooked.
  • Do not force eating, and do not use sweets as a reward.

Talk to your doctor, midwife or health visitor if

  • Your child is not growing or gaining weight, or seems unwell.
  • Your child eats only a very small number of foods.
  • Meal times are causing a lot of worry for you.

This guide is general information, not medical advice. Trust your instinct. If you are worried, contact your doctor, midwife or health visitor. This guide is checked against the NHS, the World Health Organization (WHO), the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) and other public health sources.
