Screens, games and a first phone

There is no single right amount of screen time for school-age children. What matters most is that screens do not push out sleep, activity, homework and time together.

What is common

  • Experts suggest clear family limits rather than one number of hours for every child.
  • Games, videos and apps are designed to keep children watching. Stopping is hard, and that is normal.
  • There is no right age for a first phone. It depends on your child, your family and why the phone is needed.

What can help

  • Make a family media plan together: when, where and how long screens are used.
  • Keep screens out of meals and the hour before bed. Charge devices outside the bedroom at night.
  • Check the age rating of games and apps before your child uses them.
  • Play or watch together sometimes, and ask your child to show you their favourite game.
  • Give a 5 minute warning before screen time ends.
  • If you choose a first phone, start with a simple phone or limited features, use parental controls and agree the rules before you hand it over.
  • Show the habits you want to see. Put your own phone away at meals and family time.

Talk to your doctor, midwife or health visitor if

  • Screens are causing a lot of conflict at home.
  • Your child seems unable to stop, gets very upset without screens, or gives up friends, sleep or activities for them.
  • You are worried about your child's mood, sleep or schoolwork.

This guide is general information, not medical advice. Trust your instinct. If you are worried, contact your doctor, midwife or health visitor. This guide is checked against the NHS, the World Health Organization (WHO), the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) and other public health sources.
