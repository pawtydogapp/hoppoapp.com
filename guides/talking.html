My child is not talking much yet

Children learn to talk at different ages. Everyday chat is the best practice there is.

What is common

  • Around 15 months, many children try 1 or 2 words other than mama or dada.
  • Around 18 months, many try 3 or more words and follow simple requests.
  • Around 2 years, many put 2 words together, like "more milk".
  • Children often understand much more than they can say.
  • Children who hear 2 languages at home learn both. Keep using your home languages.

What can help

  • Name things as you do everyday jobs: getting dressed, cooking, shopping.
  • Watch where your child looks and talk about what they see.
  • Give simple choices: "Apple or banana?"
  • Read picture books and sing songs with actions.
  • Wait a few seconds after you ask something, so your child has time to answer.
  • Avoid screens for children under 2. When you do use them, watch together and talk about what you see.

Talk to your doctor, midwife or health visitor if

  • By about 12 months, your child does not use sounds or gestures to ask for things, or does not play games like peekaboo.
  • Your child has no words by about 18 months, or does not point or wave.
  • Your child does not put 2 words together by about 2 years.
  • Your child does not seem to understand simple words or requests.
  • Your child loses words or skills they had before.
  • You are worried about your child's hearing. A hearing check is quick and easy to arrange.

This guide is general information, not medical advice. Trust your instinct. If you are worried, contact your doctor, midwife or health visitor. This guide is checked against the NHS, the World Health Organization (WHO), the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) and other public health sources.
