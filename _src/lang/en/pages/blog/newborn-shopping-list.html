Newborn shopping list: the 8 things you need before the birth, and what parents wish they had skipped

The lists start arriving in the middle of pregnancy, from friends, shops and apps, and the longest run to nearly 50 items. Many parents later say they bought things they never used, and still underestimated how many nappies they would need. Here is a short list sorted by when to buy, the buys parents most often regret, the products never to use for sleep, and a traffic light for second-hand kit.

The best-known list has 48 items. Parents say you need about 8 first

One of the biggest UK parenting websites lists 41 "essentials" plus 7 nice-to-haves. It is an advertisement feature for a supermarket, with affiliate links, and has no "do not buy" list.

In a long Mumsnet thread from 2020 about what first-time parents do not need, the answers kept coming back to the same short core: somewhere safe to sleep, a car seat, nappies, a few sleepsuits and plenty of muslins.

Surveys point the same way. In research commissioned by the supermarket Aldi in the UK (January 2024), 30% of parents said they bought supposed essentials before their first child and never used them. In the same survey, 62% underestimated how many nappies and wipes they would need. Many families overbuy the extras and underbuy the basics.

The 8 things to have ready before the birth

Have these ready a few weeks before your due date, in case your baby arrives early.

#ItemHow many, and what to check
1A safe sleep space: cot, bedside cot or Moses basketA firm, flat mattress that fits with no gaps, and a few fitted sheets. The Lullaby Trust says the head should sink no more than a few millimetres
2A rear-facing car seatYou need it to bring your baby home by car. Buy it new, look for the R129 (i-Size) label and practise fitting it in your own car
3Nappies and wipes, or cotton wool and waterSome newborn size, not a cupboard full. Babies grow out of the smallest size quickly, so buy more once you know what fits
46 to 8 sleepsuits and 6 to 8 vestsMostly in 0 to 3 months. Long babies can skip newborn size entirely
5Muslin clothsMore than you think, say parents on Mumsnet, for spills, burps and everything else
6A baby sleeping bag or cellular blanketsMany sleeping bags are sized from about 4 kg (Lullaby Trust), so a small newborn may need cellular blankets first, tucked in no higher than the shoulders
7A pram, pushchair or carrierTry before you buy if you can
8Feeding basics for your planFor formula: bottles, teats, a way to sterilise and first infant formula. For breastfeeding: very little at first, perhaps breast pads and a comfortable bra

If money or space is tight, start here. The sleep space, the car seat and what goes into the cot matter more than anything else on this page. Our hospital bag guide covers what to pack for the birth itself.

In the first weeks: buy once you have met your baby

These depend on your baby's size, temperament and feeding:

  • More nappies, in the size that actually fits
  • The next clothes size up
  • A changing mat you can move from room to room
  • A bouncer for awake time
  • A breast pump, if you turn out to need one
  • A white noise app or machine, if your baby settles better with it

Depends on your baby: borrow or try first. Some items top 1 parent's "best" list and another parent's "worst" list. In a survey of about 1,400 readers by the economist Emily Oster (ParentData, 2024), the feeding pillow was named best as often as worst. Bouncers, monitors and play gyms appeared on both lists. Oster also suggests trial sets of swaddles, dummies and bottles, because babies differ, and notes that items such as a Moses basket may get used for almost no time. Borrow these, or buy them second hand, once you know your baby.

7 buys parents often call a waste of money

  1. Warmers of any kind. Warmers, for anything, were on the ParentData worst list and not on the best list.
  2. Fancy outfits. Outfits were on the ParentData worst list. They are also a popular gift, so you will probably get plenty anyway.
  3. Tiny shoes. In the Aldi research, 30% of buyers never or hardly used them, at an average cost of £33.
  4. Scratch mittens and dressing gowns. Over a quarter of buyers said they were barely used.
  5. A special nappy bin. 1 Mumsnet parent called it a complete waste of money. A lidded bin and nappy bags do the job.
  6. A baby bath. Many parents use the sink, a washing-up bowl or the big bath with a parent in it.
  7. A changing table. A portable mat often gets more use.

The surprise favourites: what parents would buy again

  • A carrier or sling. It was the single most-named "best" item in the ParentData survey, and 1 of the most recommended buys in the 2018 Mumsnet thread. The NHS says never let the fabric cover your baby's head.
  • Baby sleeping bags. They scored very well with parents and the NHS lists a baby sleeping bag as an option alongside lightweight blankets and sheets.
  • White noise. 1 Mumsnet parent called an app the best money she ever spent.
  • A bouncer, for awake time only. Not for sleep.
  • A small spatula for nappy cream. The surprise hit of the ParentData survey.

Never for sleep: products to leave on the shelf

Some of these are sold as baby sleep products. Safe sleep bodies in the UK and the US advise against all of them for sleep, at every nap and every night.

ProductWhyWho says so
Sleep pods, nests and positionersSoft, raised or cushioned sides, and they hold a baby in one positionNHS, Lullaby Trust
Cot bumpersA serious risk to babies. Padded bumpers are banned from sale in the USLullaby Trust, American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP), US Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC)
Inclined sleepers, and sleeping in bouncers, swings or car seatsA seated or inclined position can restrict breathing. The AAP says no sleep surface should incline more than 10 degreesLullaby Trust, AAP
Weighted sleeping bags, swaddles and blanketsThe AAP says nothing weighted on or near a sleeping babyAAP, CPSC, Lullaby Trust
Pillows, duvets and quiltsShown to raise the risk of sudden infant death syndrome (SIDS) about 3 timesNHS, Lullaby Trust
Baby hammocksNot a flat sleep surfaceLullaby Trust

A safety label does not make a product safe for sleep. The Lullaby Trust points out that a product can meet a standard and still not follow safer sleep advice. If your baby falls asleep in a carrier, swing or car seat, the AAP says to move them to a firm, flat sleep surface on their back as soon as you can. Our safe sleep guide covers the full set of rules.

New, used or borrowed? A traffic light for second-hand kit

Most second-hand kit is fine. In a 2024 Which? survey, 89% of parents with a second-hand pushchair were satisfied. 2 items are different.

LightItemsWhat to check
Red: buy newCar seatA seat can have hidden damage from a crash or a drop. The Royal Society for the Prevention of Accidents (RoSPA) says do not buy one second hand, and accept one only from family or friends, and only if you know its full history, it has the original instructions and it is not too old. The Lullaby Trust goes further and advises against using any second-hand car seat
Red: buy newCot or Moses basket mattressThe Lullaby Trust recommends a new mattress for each baby. If you reuse your own, it should be firm, flat, clean, dry, free of rips or water damage, fit with no gaps and have a waterproof cover and a fire-resistant label
Amber: fine with checksCot and travel cotAn EN 716 label, no broken or missing parts and no loose stickers. Gaps between bars of 4.5 to 6.5 cm, the range the Swiss Council for Accident Prevention (BFU) gives in line with EN 716. Beware paint on cots made before 1973
Amber: fine with checksPram or pushchairAn EN 1888 label, working brakes, a smooth fold with no finger traps, and no recalls. Do not take a used car seat with a travel system
Amber: fine with checksStair gates, slings, monitorsGates: an EN 1930 label. Slings: no tears or loose stitching, and the instructions. Monitors: a new password and software updates
Green: usually fineClothes, muslins, bottles, breast pump parts, most toysNo drawstrings or loose buttons, no novelty sleeping bags with hoods. Sterilise anything that goes in a mouth. Check toys for button batteries and magnets

Car seat rules changed in 2024. Since 1 September 2024, seats approved only to the older R44 rules can no longer be sold in the European Union (EU). Seats already in use stay legal, according to Ireland's Road Safety Authority. Combined with the hidden damage risk, this is 1 more reason to buy the car seat new.

Mesh-sided travel cots: advice differs. In the US, play yards that meet federal safety rules are accepted for sleep, and US guidance says to use only the mattress sold with them. Slovenia's National Institute of Public Health (NIJZ) advises against folding cots with taut mesh sides because of the injury risk. Hoppo suggests a travel cot for trips, with only its own mattress, and a barred cot with an EN 716 label for every night at home.

You will never feel ready, but you are

The Aldi research found that 78% of parents needed the same or more emotional support than they expected. Many parents look back and say what they lacked was help, not stuff.

When family ask what to buy, point them to nappies in the next size up, or to a meal. For a fuller picture of spending, see what a baby really costs in the first year.

The short version

  • Before the birth: a safe sleep space with a new mattress, a car seat, nappies, 6 to 8 sleepsuits and vests, muslins, a sleeping bag or blankets, a pram or carrier and feeding basics.
  • Sleep space, car seat and safe sleep matter more than anything else on the list.
  • Never for sleep: pods, nests, bumpers, positioners, inclined sleepers, weighted products, pillows and duvets.
  • Buy the car seat and mattress new. Most other kit is fine second hand with checks.
